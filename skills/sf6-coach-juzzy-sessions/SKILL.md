# Sit Down and Shut Up sessions and set reviews (Coach Juzzy)

Source: Juzzy (YouTube @juzz87, https://www.youtube.com/@juzz87), a 1600 MR Ken who runs "Sit Down and Shut Up" (SDASU) on the FightRise Discord (fightrise.com). In each session a student plays him an FT3, they do a live replay review and lab work, then run a second FT3 to see whether the changes stick. This skill paraphrases recurring themes from the wrap-up of 89 full session VODs (Ep. 1-89, students from Gold to 1800 MR) and four full set reviews. Short lessons cut from the sessions are in sf6-coach-juzzy-tips. The sf6-replay-analysis skill loads this for every review. Shared with Juzzy's permission.

**Coverage:** the set reviews and the wrap-up of each session were read; the middle of each session (the live review and lab) was only searched for themes. Quote episode numbers when citing, and treat a theme as a session-level summary rather than a moment-by-moment account.

## How Juzzy coaches
- Start from what the student wants to fix, and check during the session that it makes sense to them (Ep. 50, his talk on coaching at FightRise).
- Most players at these levels lose to "one or two habits" that the opponent is exploiting (Ep. 83). Find those first.
- End with one or two actionable items, then run it back to test them. Lab-only items (combo routes, oki sequences) are homework, not things to fix mid-set (Ep. 82).
- Results in the runback matter less than whether the texture of the game changed, e.g. winning more ground interactions (Ep. 8).

## Neutral
- **Play grounded; pokes before specials.** Do less with special moves and more with pokes, even non-cancelable ones. Effective pokes make the opponent respect your range, which exposes their movement and opens up 2MK~Drive Rush and whiff punishes (Ep. 8, Modern Cammy: more 2MK~Drive Rushes landed once the pokes landed).
- **Don't give up space for free.** Walking back is often what gets players clipped. Right after blocking a hit is a poor time to back up (Ep. 2), "try not to back up so much" (Ep. 22, Cammy), and don't hand over space for nothing (Ep. 75).
- **Never whiff.** The most repeated single instruction: in the runback, focus only on landing your button, on hit or block (Ep. 60, 63, 72, 86). See the drill in sf6-coach-juzzy-tips.
- **Check Drive Rush instead of guessing after it.** A game plan that depends on guessing right at −2 after Drive Rush jab is volatile; checking the Drive Rush from the spacing it comes from is stable (Marisa v Luke set review).
- **Below Master, stop the neutral skips.** Anti-air the jumps, check Dragonlash, scissors, headbutt, Blanka ball and Drive Rush, walk forward and parry fireballs to get to where they can't throw them, and you win most games (Akuma v Ken set review).

## Corner
- **Park in front of the cornered opponent, under the timer.** Stand just outside their longest normals (e.g. Ken 5HK range), walk in and out, and whiff-punish what they press. Every character struggles there. "You win in the corners", and Cammy is good at getting there (Ep. 82).
- **Getting out of the corner** is easier once you can press your buttons effectively, using low-commitment pokes rather than a high dive kick that gets fully punished (Ep. 8).
- In the corner with a life and meter lead, see "Overextending" in sf6-coach-juzzy-tips.

## Offense
- **Enforce plus frames first, then layer.** On oki, use the setups you have; with plus frames, enforce them until they stop mashing, then mix in other options (Cammy v JP set review). Lights on oki are still strong; vary the light chain and leave gaps as you meet people who delay-button after your jabs (Ep. 82).
- **Oki options should cover several defensive options.** Options that beat only a narrow set are reads and need a reason (Marisa v Luke set review).
- **Don't hard-commit where a button does the same job.** Stop mashing Spin Knuckle at spacings where a walk forward and a button would also get space or a blocked hit with less risk (Cammy v JP set review).
- **Keep using what works.** A dive kick used as a whiff punish made the JP hesitant to press, and then the student stopped using it (Cammy v JP). Conversely, don't change a game plan that's working without a reason (Marisa v Luke).
- **Adapt after the first answer.** When Luke jumped the first SPD attempt from plus frames, the second identical attempt was jumped too. After one escape, assume they'll do it again: keep the plus frames tight to catch the jump, or wait and anti-air it (Zangief v Luke set review).
- **Don't autopilot a preemptive button after a knockdown.** If you're whiff punishing a wake-up button, hitting it on block is better than whiffing outright (Marisa v Luke).

## Punishes and damage
- **Have damage behind your shimmy.** Delay tech is everywhere around 1500 MR. In Diamond any combo after a shimmy is enough; around 1500, know your meterless, 2-bar and 3-bar shimmy punish routes and their damage, so you control how many interactions the round takes (Ep. 23, Modern Cammy: watch out for the Modern auto input turning the knuckle into OD).
- **Count interactions to kill, not life.** Higher up, an opponent with full Drive and super is one interaction away from killing you. Before cashing out, ask how much damage you want, how many interactions are left and whether you can win from here (Ep. 23).
- **Cash out.** Missed kills and weak punishes on blocked Drive Impacts swung games (Cammy v JP set review). Pair with "Just Use Super" in sf6-coach-juzzy-tips.

## Defense
- **Block more.** Try not to press right away under pressure; block it out and see what happens (Ep. 66). At D2, reversal a little more, delay tech a little less and block more, because shimmies start appearing at that rank (Ep. 71).
- **Add delay options.** Delay tech, and delay button against Drive Rush jab or jab-jab-throw where you're minus and walking back is hard (Ep. 42, Marisa v Luke). Where a string has a gap (e.g. a gapped 2MK), a DP or Drive Reversal option select (DPOS) can take the turn back (Ep. 82).
- **Don't burn out and don't reversal too often**, even when reversal is the character's only consistent way out (Ep. 42).
- **Taking the throw when it kills is a different decision.** If the throw is lethal it stops being a 1,200-damage baseline, so be willing to act (tech, reversal, jump) and accept the occasional shimmy (Cammy v JP; Ep. 23). When every option loses anyway, a reversal super is the risk to take (Akuma v Ken).
- **Block on wake-up** in ordinary situations instead of pressing (Akuma v Ken: the Ken lost rounds refusing to block).
- **Stop second-guessing.** Go with what you think will work, and watch your meter (Ep. 43).

## Drive and meter
- **Don't Drive Rush cancel so much** (Ep. 82, the one in-set item for a 1222 MR Cammy).
- **With low Drive, play Cammy's buttons.** Take meterless conversions, use throws and normal pressure, and the Drive comes back (Ep. 22). Desperation Drive Rushes when you'd die anyway are fine.
- **A burnt-out player who goes passive** gets walk-up thrown and chipped (Zangief v Luke set review).
- Combo routing and meter spending are the first fixes for a player new to a character (Akuma v Ken).

## Set reviews (one line each)
- **Zangief v Luke** (FT2 bracket, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AN2ru4utqPY): break the autopilot on command normals (the knee), practise disjointed buttons to their tip range, and adapt once the opponent shows they jump the SPD.
- **Marisa v Luke** (FT5, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qjx2DKatQLo): check Drive Rush rather than guessing at −2, oki that covers several options, delay options on defense, no autopiloted wake-up buttons.
- **Cammy v JP** (FT2 ranked, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NjMIfTQvl9E): cash outs, stop mashing Spin Knuckle, enforce plus frames on oki, better punishes on Drive Impact, keep the dive kick whiff punish, matchup knowledge (fake portal setups, punishing light spins), and taking a lethal throw.
- **Akuma v Ken** (FT2 ranked, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XekQ5B5AQFU): stop neutral skips, block on wake-up, check Drive Rush from range, use normals to take space, then fix combo routing and meter.

## How to apply this in a replay review
1. **One or two habits:** after the standard checks, name the one or two habits that cost {{PLAYER_NAME}} the most, the way Juzzy would end a session, and make them the top fixes.
2. **Walking back:** log hits {{PLAYER_NAME}} took while walking back, especially right after blocking. Note the space he gave up and whether holding his ground or a poke was available.
3. **Grounded vs special moves:** count {{PLAYER_NAME}}'s committal specials in neutral (Spin Knuckle, Hooligan, high dive kicks) against his pokes. If specials dominate and get punished, recommend the grounded plan.
4. **Corner:** when {{PLAYER_NAME}} has the opponent cornered, did he park outside their longest normal and whiff-punish, or overextend? When cornered himself, did he use low-commitment buttons to get out?
5. **Shimmy punishes and cash-outs:** record the damage of each shimmy punish and each missed kill, and compare with the interactions left in the round. Suggest the route (meterless, 2-bar, 3-bar) that fits.
6. **Defense:** flag presses under pressure where blocking was fine, reversals that burnt him out, and lethal throws he took without acting.
7. **Adaptation:** when one of {{PLAYER_NAME}}'s options was escaped, did he repeat it unchanged? When something worked (e.g. dive kick whiff punish), did he stop using it?
8. **Drive:** count pressure and combo Drive Rush cancels against burnouts and the Drive gap; recommend fewer cancels if they keep leaving him low.
