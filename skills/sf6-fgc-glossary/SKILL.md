## How to use this in reviews
- Use these terms consistently in reports, dashboards and story items, and define a term in a few words the first time it appears if the player might not use it.
- The player's own definitions win where they differ. From the sf6-review-dashboard skill:
  - **round** = one life bar;
  - **game** = one replay;
  - **set** = a group of games (FT2/FT3/FT5).
  - The glossary's "match" means a tournament set; avoid "match" for a single game.
- Many terms name review concepts the E.X. Dojo skills already cover (shimmy, spacing trap, stagger, layers, conditioning, turn, respect, check, abare). Use the glossary wording for the label and the skill for the judgement.
- Terms from other games or old Street Fighter titles are listed briefly so they can be recognised in VODs or coaching videos. Don't use them as SF6 mechanics.

## Street Fighter 6 systems
- **Drive System / Drive gauge**: the six-segment green bar under health that pays for SF6's offensive and defensive tools (Drive Impact, Drive Rush, Overdrive moves, Drive Parry, Drive Reversal).
- **Drive Impact (DI)**: HP+HK, 1 bar. A long-reaching armored strike. If it absorbs a hit and connects, or lands as a punish counter, the opponent crumples into a full combo. Blocking it midscreen is fine; blocking it in the corner causes a wall splat.
- **Drive Parry**: MP+MK. A stance that blocks everything (projectiles too). It costs half a bar up front and keeps draining while held, and it refunds Drive when it catches hits. A throw beats it.
- **Perfect Parry (PP)**: parrying within about 2 frames before a hit lands (holding back, or down-back for lows, against strikes). The screen freezes and you recover almost at once for a big punish; projectiles and late parries are "imperfect".
- **Drive Rush (DR)**: a green dash, either from a parry (MP+MK then forward-forward, 1 bar: "parry rush" / "raw drive rush") or by cancelling a cancellable normal into it (3 bars: "drive rush cancel"). The next normal gets extra frame advantage.
- **Drive Reversal**: forward + HP+HK while blocking (or on wake-up), 2 bars. An invincible counter-attack that pushes the opponent away and does recoverable damage.
- **Burnout**: the state after the Drive gauge empties. The bar reads EMPTY and the character turns grey. You can't use any Drive tools, you take chip from blocked specials and you're stunned by a blocked Drive Impact in the corner. The gauge slowly refills.
- **Overdrive (OD) move**: a special done with two punches or two kicks for 2 Drive bars, with better properties (invincibility, speed, launch). Older games call these EX moves.
- **Super Art (SA1/SA2/SA3)**: SF6's three super levels.
- **Critical Art (CA)**: the Level 3 super done below 25% health (yellow health bar). Same cost, extra animation and more damage than a normal SA3.
- **Punish Counter (PC)**: hitting the opponent in the recovery of a move. It does 20% more damage, adds 4 frames of hitstun and drains some of their Drive.
- **Forced Knockdown**: hitting certain airborne specials (for example a dive or a horizontal ball) with a normal causes a knockdown that can be juggled, instead of a flip-out.
- **Classic / Modern / Dynamic controls**: Classic is the six-button layout with motion and charge inputs. Modern has light/medium/heavy plus a special button and assisted inputs. Dynamic is an AI-assisted three-button mode for casual play.

## Street Fighter names, moves and archetypes
- **Shoto**: a character with a fireball, a DP and a tatsu (Ryu, Ken, Akuma), whose plan is fireballs and footsies, then DP when you jump.
- **Hadouken**: Ryu and Ken's fireball; loosely, any forward motion-input projectile.
- **Shoryuken (shoryu, SRK, uppercut)**: Ryu and Ken's DP; loosely, any uppercut-style invincible anti-air.
- **Tatsu (hurricane kick)**: a shoto's spinning forward kick, often briefly projectile-invincible and a good combo ender.
- **Flash kick**: Guile's down-up charge anti-air/reversal; loosely, any down-up charge flip kick.
- **Sonic Boom**: Guile's charge projectile with short recovery; loosely, any fast-recovering projectile.
- **SPD (Spinning Piledriver)**: Zangief's 360 command throw; loosely, any 360 grab. Fast, damaging and long range.
- **Running Bear Grab**: a command grab that runs forward. Slower and weaker than an SPD.
- **Drill**: a low, lunging, spinning attack. Cammy's Spiral Arrow is the classic example.
- **Sobat**: a spinning horizontal kick (Guile's command normal, Dee Jay's special).
- **Target combo (TC)**: a character-specific chain of normals that cancel into each other.
- **Magic series**: chaining normals light → medium → heavy (a system in some games, not SF6).
- **Mexican uppercut**: old slang for a Ryu 2HP-style upward punch normal used as an anti-air.
- **Demon Flip**: Akuma's command jump with dive kick, low and throw options.
- **Raging Demon**: Akuma's cinematic grab super.
- **Delta motion**: an old charge-super input ([1]319).
- **Cowardcopter**: air tatsu used to fly out of the corner.
- **Naming swap**: Boxer = Balrog (M. Bison in Japan), Claw = Vega (Balrog in Japan), Dictator = M. Bison (Vega in Japan), Gouki = Akuma.

## Older Street Fighter mechanics (recognise only)
- **Alpha Counter**: Alpha-series guard cancel; the ancestor of the Drive Reversal (people say "alpha counter" for any guard cancel).
- **ISM**: Alpha 3 meter modes; V-ISM gives custom combos.
- **Custom combo / Valle CC / Crouch Cancel infinite**: Alpha-era free-cancel modes and the exploits found in them.
- **Focus Attack / FADC / Red Focus / Four Finger Tech**: SF4's armored absorb, its dash cancel (used to make DPs safe or extend combos), the multi-hit version, and a throw-tech plus focus option select.
- **Parry / Red Parry / SGGK / DED OS / UOH / Genei Jin, Daipan loop, Keeper Jin**: SF3:3S mechanics, option selects and Yun's install and its combos.
- **V-Trigger / V-Skill / V-Reversal / V-Shift / V-Shift Break / Crush Counter**: SF5's meter system and its heavy counter-hit state.
- **Groove / Ratio / Roll cancel / Paint the Fence**: CvS2 team systems, its invincible-special bug and a Bison loop.
- **Gems / Pandora**: SFxT team buffs and a comeback mode.
- **Excel**: SF EX2's free-cancel mode.
- **Renda cancel / Plink / Select plink**: older input tricks.
- **Dream Combo**: Ed's SF6 orb-bounce combo.
- **Ultra Combo**: KI's round-ending ender (the glossary files it here).

## Frame data and timing
- **Advantage / disadvantage**: who can act first after a move hits or is blocked ("plus" / "minus").
- **Turn**: the idea that the plus player "should" act and the minus player "should" block. It's a convention to exploit, not a rule.
- **Abare**: pressing a button while minus, betting the opponent won't follow up. It's a risky turn-steal.
- **Challenge**: attacking back right out of blockstun.
- **Check**: a fast, low-risk button to interrupt aggressive movement or Drive Rush at close range.
- **Hit stun**: the frames you can't act after being hit.
- **Hitstop / blockstop**: the brief freeze on contact, which doesn't count toward advantage.
- **Absolute guard**: you stay in blockstun through a true blockstring even if you let go of block.
- **Tight / real / fake**: a tight string has no gaps; a "real" situation has no reliable escape, while a fake one does.
- **1-frame link / just frame**: an input with a one-frame window.
- **Buffer window**: the frames where an early input is stored and comes out on the first possible frame (reversals, for example).
- **Magic number**: the frame value that splits moves into punishable and safe, or plus enough to frame trap (e.g. -4 in a game with 4f jabs).
- **Mental frame advantage**: cancelling a minus move into a frame trap so the "turn" is a bluff.
- **Throw protection**: brief throw invincibility after blockstun or wake-up.
- **Frame kill**: whiffing moves on purpose to time a meaty or safe jump.
- **Autotimed / manually timed**: oki that comes from acting as soon as possible, versus oki that needs eyeballed spacing or timing.
- **Set play**: a planned, timed setup after a knockdown.
- **Safejab**: a grounded meaty that recovers in time to block a reversal; the grounded cousin of a safe jump.
- **Window**: the time available to do something.
- **Jump test**: a training-mode trick for estimating how plus a move is.

## Offense
- **Pressure**: repeated close attacks with safe strings and frame advantage.
- **Bullying**: forcing blocks over and over with the same plus or safe move.
- **Tick throw**: a light blocked normal then a throw.
- **Throw loop**: being close enough after a throw to throw again on wake-up. "Take the throw" is often the defender's best answer.
- **Shimmy**: walking into throw range then back out so a tech whiffs, then punishing it.
- **Spacing trap**: a minus move done at a range where the opponent's retaliation whiffs, then punishing the whiff.
- **Mixup / layered mixup / layers**: guesses the defender must make. Layers are the backup plans behind the first mixup, and "levels" of the mind game.
- **Vortex**: a mixup that leads to a knockdown that loops into the same mixup.
- **Ambiguous**: a jump-in that may or may not cross up.
- **Fuzzy attack**: hitting a just-crouched character with a move that only hits standing hurtboxes.
- **Cross-under / corner steal / corpse hop / side switch**: ways to swap sides, usually to escape or take the corner.
- **Empty jump / empty jump low / empty throw**: jumping without an air attack, then going low or throwing.
- **Instant overhead**: an air attack right after leaving the ground.
- **Party starter**: the hit or knockdown that starts a character's best offense.
- **Neutral skip**: a fast, usually safe move that crosses long distance into offense.
- **Fish**: repeating a rewarding poke with a buffered follow-up, hoping it hits.
- **Buffered attack**: whiffing a normal with a cancel buffered, so the special only comes out if it touches.
- **Raw**: doing a move with nothing before it (raw DI, raw super).
- **Conversion**: turning a stray hit into a combo.
- **Hit confirm types**:
  - crouch confirm: checking that the opponent is crouching for a crouch-only route;
  - twitch confirm: confirming because you saw the opponent move or press during your attack.
- **Represent**: showing you'll use an option so the opponent has to respect it later.
- **Respect**: pre-emptively defending against an option you expect.
- **Gimmick / knowledge check**: plays that only work if the opponent doesn't know the answer.
- **Win condition**: the situation your character wants to reach to win (a knockdown, the corner, meter).

## Defense
- **Down back**: blocking, or sitting still holding block.
- **Turtling**: a heavily defensive style.
- **Fuzzy guard**: switching block direction very precisely to cover two options (high/low, left/right).
- **Delayed wakeup**: staying down slightly longer to ruin timing; not in SF6.
- **Quick rise / back roll**: standing up immediately, or rolling back, from a soft knockdown.
- **Soft / hard knockdown**: a soft knockdown gives wake-up options; a hard knockdown fixes the time on the ground.
- **Pre-jump**: the throw-invincible startup frames of a jump.
- **Trip guard**: blocking right after landing from an empty jump.
- **Crouch tech**: a crouching throw-tech attempt that becomes a low normal if no throw comes; mostly older games.
- **Checkmate**: no option avoids the loss (for example a safe jump at a sliver of life).
- **Proximity block**: holding back near an attack makes you stop and block instead of walking away.
- **Reactable / unreactable**: whether startup is slow enough for a human to answer on reaction (jumps and DI yes, most normals no).
- **Mental stack**: how many things a player is watching for at once; more means slower reactions.
- **Pushback**: how far a hit or block pushes the attacker away. It decides whether a minus move is actually punishable at that range.

## Neutral and spacing
- **Range** (close / mid / long) **and space / spacing**: where the players are and which area each move controls.
- **Poke / counter poke**: a far-reaching, safe normal that holds space, and hitting someone's poke with your own.
- **Stop sign**: an especially strong poke or air normal that stops approaches.
- **Whiff / whiff cancel**: missing completely, and cancelling a move that missed.
- **Wish punish**: swinging at empty space hoping to whiff-punish, rather than reacting to an actual whiff.
- **Bulldogging**: walking forward and blocking to slowly close in on a zoner.
- **Fireball war / fireball trap**: trading projectiles, and spacing fireballs so a cornered opponent can't jump out.
- **Zoning / zoner, runaway, hit and run**: different ways to keep away or chip from range.
- **Anti-air / air-to-air**: hitting a jumper from the ground, or meeting them in the air.
- **Air reset / flipout**: an air hit that lands the opponent on their feet, sometimes into a mixup as they land.
- **Grappler jump**: a slow character's empty jump from a specific range.
- **Deep**: a jump-in that hits very low on the body (close to landing).
- **Stale**: a hit very late in a move's active frames with changed properties (a weak late DP).
- **Trade / stuff**: both hitting on the same frame, and beating an attack before it comes out.

## Hitboxes and move properties
- **Hitbox / hurtbox / pushbox**: the hitting area, the hittable area, and the collision area that keeps characters apart.
- **Disjointed hitbox**: a hitbox far from any hurtbox, so it's hard to whiff-punish.
- **Armor / armor break / guard point**: absorbing hits while attacking; moves that ignore armor; blocking automatically during part of a move.
- **Invincibility types**:
  - strike invincible: throws still win;
  - throw invincible: strikes still win;
  - projectile invincible.
- **Crush and profile terms**:
  - high crush: avoids high or upper-body attacks;
  - low crush: avoids lows;
  - low profile: a hurtbox low enough to go under mid attacks;
  - high profile: lifts the hurtbox over lows.
- **Projectile / trap / beam**: a hitbox moving on its own; a lingering one; a near-instant full-screen one.
- **Unblockable / unblockable protection**: an attack that can't be blocked, and systems that prevent cheap two-way unblockables.
- **Hit throw**: a blockable strike that animates like a throw on hit.
- **Vacuum**: an attack that pulls the opponent in.
- **Sway / teleport / slide / sweep**:
  - sway: dodges back and then attacks;
  - teleport: an invincible reposition;
  - slide: a low that travels along the ground;
  - sweep: a knockdown low, usually 2HK.
- **Crumple**: long collapse hitstun (Drive Impact, SF4 focus).
- **Wall splat / wall bounce**: sticking to or bouncing off the screen edge.
- **Force stand / restand**: making a crouching or airborne opponent standing again, so a combo or extra advantage works.
- **Proximity normal**: a close version of a button that only comes out point blank.

## Combos and damage
- **Starter / filler / ender, route**: the first hit (which sets the scaling), the middle, the finish, and the chosen path through them.
- **Extension / follow-up**: adding more hits, often with meter, and optional extra presses after certain moves.
- **Optimal / tournament combo**: the maximum-value route, versus the reliable one you'll land under pressure.
- **Meterless / build meter**: combos without meter, and deliberate meter gain.
- **Juggle potential / gravity scaling / hit stun deterioration / guts scaling**: systems that limit long combos or reduce damage at low health.
- **Touch of death / infinite / loop**: combos that kill from one hit, combos that never end in theory, and repeated sequences.
- **Recoverable (white) life**: damage that can heal back; Drive Reversal and chip in SF6 create it.
- **Stun / dizzy, redizzy**: being stunned from too much damage, and stunning someone again straight after.
- **Corner carry**: how far a combo pushes the opponent toward the corner.
- **Counter / catch counter**: shorthand for counter hit, and moves that catch an attack and strike back.
- **Character specific**: a combo that only works on some characters.

## Inputs and execution
- **Motion inputs**:
  - quarter and half circle (HCF/HCB);
  - DP motion 623;
  - 360 and 720;
  - negative edge (a special on button release);
  - motion input as a general term.
- **Execution tricks**:
  - plink and piano: pressing buttons in quick succession;
  - double tap: pressing the same button twice fast;
  - kara cancel and kara throw: cancelling a move's startup, often to extend throw range;
  - buffered motion: doing the motion early, then pressing on reaction;
  - charge buffering and charge partitioning;
  - auto-correct and cross-cut: DP input tricks when someone crosses over;
  - longcut: a deliberately long input;
  - macro;
  - SOCD (leverless left+right handling).
- **Execution, skill floor, skill ceiling**: physical input skill, the entry bar, and the top-end limit.
- **Input lag, netcode (rollback / delay-based), GGPO**: online and display delay terms.

## Mind games and player profile
- **Conditioning**: building a pattern so a later change catches the opponent.
- **Bait**: inviting a response you're ready to punish.
- **Download**: learning the opponent's habits mid-set.
- **Mind game / yomi / psychic**: reading the opponent, and reads so good they look like mind-reading.
- **RPS**: rock-paper-scissors option sets, e.g. strike beats throw, throw beats block, block beats strike. Pairs with the chess-or-poker rule in full-review-report.
- **Risk / reward**: what can go wrong versus what you gain; judge choices by both.
- **Flowchart / autopilot**: fixed if-then behaviour, often without thinking, which the opponent can bait.
- **Random / mash / degenerate / gimmick**:
  - random: unpredictable play;
  - mash: thoughtless button presses;
  - degenerate: one strategy only;
  - gimmick: surprise-dependent.
- **Solid / honest / fundamentals**: low-risk play built on good basics (spacing, anti-airs, punishes, reactions).
- **Tilted / salt / scrub**: playing worse from frustration; frustration itself; someone who blames the game.
- **Comeback / robbery / magic pixel / hood perfect**:
  - comeback: winning from far behind;
  - robbery: the loser's word for a comeback;
  - magic pixel: surviving on no visible health;
  - hood perfect: a win where the loser only landed chip.

## Character archetypes
- **Archetype**: a character's playstyle, set by their best moves.
- **All-rounder**: well-rounded at every range, like shotos.
- **Rushdown**: wants to get in and attack.
- **Zoner**: keeps you out with long-range tools.
- **Brawler**: strong mid-range strikes, often with armor.
- **Gorilla**: gets in and swings wildly.
- **Pixie**: very mobile with mixups but fragile.
- **Glass cannon**: great tools but low health.
- **Big body**: a large hurtbox, often a grappler.
- **Snowball character**: gets stronger with every hit.
- **Mid dispenser**: mostly safe mid pokes.
- **Charge character**: specials use charge inputs.
- **Puppet character**: controls a second entity.
- **Joke character**: intentionally weak and silly.
- **Battery**: builds meter for later.
- **Anchor**: the last character in a team.
- These are character types. Player archetypes (aggressive, defensive, fundamentalist, balanced) are in sf6-player-archetypes-exdojo.
